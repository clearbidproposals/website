What goes on it

  • Company overview. Who you are and who you serve, in two or three sentences.
  • Core competencies. The work you do, described in the terms buyers use.
  • Differentiators. The concrete reasons a buyer should choose you over the next bidder.
  • Past performance. Recent, relevant jobs with the client, scope, dates and results.
  • Company data. UEI, CAGE code, NAICS codes, certifications, insurance and bonding, and service area.
  • Contact. The person buyers should call or email.

Questions

Will you add anything I haven't done?

No. We write only from what you tell us and from public records about your company, such as your SBA profile or website. If something is missing, we ask you instead of guessing.

Do I need to be registered in SAM.gov first?

No, you can order at any stage. If you are registered, we include your UEI and CAGE code, which federal buyers look for. Our guide to finding and bidding on government contracts in Mississippi covers registration.

Can I update it later?

Yes. You own the finished document, and the Word file lets you change it as your company grows.

How is this different from a proposal?

A capability statement introduces your company to any buyer. A proposal answers one specific solicitation. If you have a bid due, see bid proposal writing and reviews.
