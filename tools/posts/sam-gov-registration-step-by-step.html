Before a federal agency can award you a contract, your business needs an active registration in SAM.gov. It costs nothing. GSA estimates the online form takes about 45 minutes if you have everything ready. After you submit, allow two to three weeks for it to go active; the official guides give figures from 10 to 15 business days. Here is what to gather first, the order of the screens, and where registrations usually get stuck.

Unique Entity ID only, or a full registration?

SAM.gov offers two things, and they are easy to mix up.

  • A Unique Entity ID (UEI) only. SBA describes the UEI as a 12-character, alpha-numeric value. SAM.gov says you can get one without completing a registration, and you only need your legal business name and physical address to do it. But if you only get a UEI, you cannot apply directly for federal awards.
  • A full registration. SAM.gov says registration is for organizations that want to bid directly on government contracts and apply for federal assistance. Registering also assigns you a UEI. If you want to bid as a prime contractor, this is the one you need.

On SAM.gov's checklist, the registration type for contractors is called "All Awards." It covers contracts and other procurements, plus financial assistance.

Gather this before you start

Most of this list comes from SAM.gov's Entity Registration Checklist. Have these ready and the form goes much faster:

  • Legal business name and physical address. A post office box cannot be your physical address. SAM.gov validates both.
  • Date and state of incorporation.
  • Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). You also sign an IRS consent form covering your taxpayer name and address.
  • Bank details for electronic payment: account type, routing number and account number, plus a remittance name and address.
  • NAICS codes for the work you do. The checklist points to the Census Bureau's NAICS lookup. Product Service Codes (PSCs) are optional; they are listed in the PSC manual.
  • Size figures: annual receipts and number of employees.
  • Your CAGE code, if you already have one. If you don't, GSA's quick start guide says one is assigned after you submit.
  • Three points of contact. The mandatory ones are an Accounts Receivable POC, an Electronic Business POC and a Government Business POC.
  • A Login.gov account. SAM.gov says your username and password are managed by Login.gov.

The registration, step by step

This order follows GSA's Quick Start Guide for Contract Registrations, with a few details from SAM.gov's checklist.

  1. Sign in. On SAM.gov, select "Sign In," accept the terms, and sign in through Login.gov. You may be asked for a one-time security code. If you don't have a Login.gov account, create one first.
  2. Start the registration. From your Workspace, select "Register Entity," then "Start Registration." Review the "Before You Start" information and select "Continue."
  3. Choose your purpose. Select your type of entity, then the option for contracts, which begins: "I want to be able to bid on federal contracts or other procurement opportunities."
  4. Core Data. Validate your UEI information, enter your business information and TIN, create your Marketing Partner Identification Number (MPIN), enter your CAGE code if you have one, and add your general information and bank (EFT) details. Then answer the questions on executive compensation and proceedings.
  5. Assertions. Enter your NAICS codes, any PSCs and your size figures. This section also asks whether you want to join the Disaster Response Registry. If you join, you can list your construction or service bonding levels, per contract and in aggregate.
  6. Representations and Certifications. Answer the FAR provisions, plus the DFARS questions if you bid on or hold Defense contracts.
  7. Points of Contact. GSA's guide calls the Electronic Business POC essential and says to list someone with direct knowledge of the registration.
  8. Submit. After your final review, select "Submit." You should see a "Registration Submitted – Confirmation" message. GSA's guide says that if you don't see it, the registration was not submitted.

If you selected NAICS codes where you count as small, the checklist says SAM.gov will give you a link to the SBA Supplemental Page. More on why that matters below.

How long it takes

StageWhat the official guides say
Filling in the formAbout 45 minutes if you have everything ready (GSA quick start guide)
Becoming activeAt least ten business days after you submit (SAM.gov checklist); up to 10 business days (GSA's Register your business page); up to 12 to 15 business days (GSA quick start guide)
Staying activeRenew every 365 days (SAM.gov)

The official numbers don't all agree, so plan on two to three weeks. Don't wait for a solicitation you want before you start. If a bid is due in ten days and you aren't registered yet, you may run out of time.

Where registrations get stuck

  • Name and address. SAM.gov validates your entity name and address. Enter your legal name exactly as your records show it, and use a street address, not a P.O. box.
  • TIN or CAGE validation. The checklist says that if your entity fails TIN or CAGE code validation, you will get an email, and you may need to work with the IRS or CAGE to update your information before you resubmit. Make sure the name and address you enter match your IRS records.
  • No confirmation message. If the "Registration Submitted – Confirmation" message never appeared, go back and finish. It was not submitted.
  • The wrong contact person. GSA says to list someone with direct knowledge of the registration as the Electronic Business POC. Pick the person who will actually handle it.
  • A lapsed renewal. Registrations must be renewed every 365 days to stay active. Put the date on your calendar the day you go active.

It's free, and so is help

SAM.gov's checklist says there is no charge to get a UEI, to register, or to keep your registration current. You don't have to pay anyone to register you.

If you get stuck, GSA's guide points to free help from the Federal Service Desk (FSD). APEX Accelerators (formerly PTACs) are another option. SBA says they can help you register in the proper places, and you can find the one nearest you through the APEX Accelerator locator.

After you're active: the profile buyers actually read

SBA's guidance compares your SAM profile to a résumé and tells you to use accurate, descriptive terms so contracting officials can find you. For small businesses, that profile also feeds SBA's Small Business Search. An SBA-listed training by the Kansas APEX Accelerators calls it "the search engine for contracting officers," notes that only a limited set of your SAM information is shared there, and calls the capability narrative the key part of the profile. You fill in that SBA profile through the Supplemental Page link SAM.gov gives you at the end of registration.

A blank or one-line narrative gives buyers little to go on. Write it the way you would write a capability statement: what you do, where, for whom, and the certifications and codes a buyer filters by.
